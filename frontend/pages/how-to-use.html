What this app is

vibeEye is a talking camera. It sits on your phone or glasses. It can name objects, describe what is in front of you, read a page, call your family as one group, send messages to everyone, and start an emergency.

You mostly use your voice. Family can also tap buttons for you.

How to start the app

  1. Open this website in Safari on iPhone, or Chrome on Android.
  2. On the sign-in screen, choose Blind person.
  3. Create an account, or sign in with username and password.
  4. Add vibeEye to your home screen so you can open it like an app.
  5. The first time, tap Allow camera, microphone, and location, then Allow on the phone.

After that, camera, microphone, and GPS start by themselves when you open the app. They stop auto-starting only when you Sign out.

You stay signed in until Sign out.

The camera home

The whole picture is one big button. You do not hunt for a microphone icon.

  • Hold the screen, speak, then let go.
  • Double tap starts or stops object detection.

When it hears you, it says Listening. At the top: logo, profile, GPS, DET. At the bottom, six buttons from left to right: Camera, Emergency, Contacts, Profile, Plans, Settings. Walking is not a button. Say take me to a place.

Object detection

This names things in the live camera, like a person, chair, or bottle. It speaks them. A helper should download it once in Settings, on Wi‑Fi: Download YOLO to this phone.

Call the family group

Family is one group. When you call, the call rings everyone. Anyone who is free can pick up. More than one person can join. A helper opens Settings once and taps Configure calling.

On Contacts, tap Family group, then voice call or video call. Green answers. X declines that person. The call can still ring other people. Mic mutes. Camera turns video on or off.

Group messages, photo, and map

The two-people button is Contacts. The main card is Family group. Send a message, photo, or map — each one goes to everybody. That is one action at a time. New messages speak out and show a notification for you, for family, and for admin.

Walking and the map

Walking directions stay off until a helper turns them on in Settings. If they are off, the app can send your location to the group instead. Ask by voice. The second dock button is Emergency, not a map.

Emergency

The second button on the bottom is Emergency. Tap it to open Emergency chat. You can text, send a voice note, send a photo, or send a map. Call and video call are at the top. Those use the in-app video call, not a phone number. Tapping Emergency does not start a call by itself.

If a blind person says emergency or SOS, the app starts an in-app video call and sends a message, a photo, and a map to admin at once. Admin is the emergency desk. This does not replace police or ambulance on the phone.

If you want only one thing, say emergency and the action:

Settings, plans, and profile

Plans (star): Free is detection, calls, and messages. Premium is describe and read, about five dollars a month, if subscriptions are on.

Profile (person): your name, health notes, System ID for family.

Settings (gear): New update, test speaker, configure calling, download detection, speech speed, language, high contrast, walking directions, system ID and QR, Sign out.
